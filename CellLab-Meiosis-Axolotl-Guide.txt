CellLab · Meiosis axolotl narration

Use the existing axolotl and free local Kokoro af_heart voice. No network, API
key, or paid service is needed. Run from the CellLab project directory:

Tools/.kokoro-venv/bin/python Tools/generate_meiosis_audio.py

The phase descriptions and key-player definitions come directly from
MeiosisKeyPlayersView.swift. Edit those descriptions there, then rerun the
generator to update the recording and transcript. READ_PHASE_DESCRIPTION is a
silent directive; only the introduction and the live description are spoken.
The game instructions and completion explanations are exported from the actual
MeiosisSession and MeiosisPopup using Tools/export_meiosis_narration.swift.
Recordings are prefixed with meiosis so the two lessons can coexist offline.

RECORD lesson.overview | Opening · Listen
Why can siblings with the same parents look so different? A chromosome shuffle!

In cells that make eggs or sperm, DNA is copied once, then the cell divides twice. The first division separates homologous chromosomes. The second separates sister chromatids. That leaves haploid products with one chromosome set. In humans, that’s twenty-three chromosomes instead of forty-six. Mixing DNA and halving the sets? That’s meiosis! Swipe through the eight steps with me!

RECORD reference.counts | Copy once · Divide twice
Let’s follow the chromosomes in a human example. Before meiosis, one diploid cell has forty-six chromosomes in twenty-three pairs. After DNA is copied, it still has forty-six chromosomes, but now there are ninety-two chromatids. Meiosis one separates homologs. Each of the two cells has twenty-three chromosomes, each still made of two sister chromatids. There’s no extra round of DNA copying. Meiosis two separates the sisters. Each of the four haploid products has twenty-three single-chromatid chromosomes. In egg formation, the cytoplasm divides unequally, giving one large egg and small polar bodies. The four products do not become four functional eggs.
