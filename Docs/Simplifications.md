# Simplifications

- The journey teaches human nuclear RNA polymerase II transcription. It is a visual model of one synthetic teaching example, not a complete account of every transcription system.
- The DNA sequence and flanking bases are invented. Coordinates are zero-based, half-open offsets into the stored 3′ → 5′ template.
- The player performs twelve selected nucleotide matches; the same session model automatically synthesizes the intervals between them. Timing, molecular sizes, diffusion, and the number of protein copies are simplified.
- General initiation machinery assembly, cap addition, spliceosome action, cleavage, poly-A addition, alternative splicing, and export are represented as guided scene events. The chemistry and molecular structures are schematic. Histones, individual general transcription factors, CTD phosphorylation, and downstream RNA degradation are outside this lesson.
- One intron is shown. Its splicing removes that interval from RNA while leaving DNA unchanged. The 5′ cap stays with the message; poly-A is added to the upstream RNA only after cleavage.
- The rendering simplifies DNA and RNA paths to keep the active site legible. DNA is paired outside the transcription bubble, and RNA is represented as a separate strand with a short hybrid inside polymerase.
- Completing this activity does not mark the full transcription lesson complete in SwiftData.
- Processing is presented sequentially for learning, although some RNA processing begins during transcription. The alternative-splicing comparison is a schematic that retains exons in their original order.
